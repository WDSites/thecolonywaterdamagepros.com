Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in The Colony, TX

Water in a kitchen soaks cabinets, subfloor and flooring at the same time, and most of it hides behind the doors. We find it, dry what can be saved and explain what cannot.

You open the cabinet under the sink and the particleboard floor feels like wet cardboard. Or the vinyl plank near the dishwasher has lifted at one seam. Kitchen water damage restoration in The Colony, TX is the work of finding every wet surface in the room, drying what can be dried and replacing what cannot, so the cabinets, subfloor and flooring end up sound again.

A kitchen is different from a bathroom or a hallway because so many materials meet in a small space. That is why one small leak can cost more than it looks like at first glance.

Where kitchen water comes from

Most kitchen losses start with a fitting that was fine for years and then was not. The usual suspects are short and easy to name:

  • Braided supply lines under the sink, which can split at the crimp or the nut.
  • The dishwasher inlet, drain hose and door gasket.
  • The refrigerator water line that feeds the ice maker and dispenser.
  • The P-trap and drain arm under a sink or disposal.
  • Caulk gaps and seams around a countertop or backsplash.

The cause matters for your claim and for who repairs it, but the room does not care. Water follows gravity into the toe kick, under the cabinet boxes and along the subfloor. For a closer look at the appliance side of these leaks, see our page on appliance leak water damage.

Signs the damage is bigger than the puddle

A small wet spot on the floor often means a larger wet area underneath. Water runs along the subfloor until it finds a low spot, a seam or a plumbing penetration, and then it spreads.

Watch for a swollen or dark cabinet floor, a toe kick that has softened or pulled away, and a faint sour smell when you open the doors. Tile that sounds hollow, grout that darkens, vinyl planks that peak at the edges and hardwood that cups are all signs that moisture is moving under the surface.

If the water bill climbed and nobody explains it, the leak may have been running for a while. A damp cabinet back wall is a strong clue.

Kitchens mix sealed hard surfaces with porous layers underneath. The top layer can look fine while the layer below is soaked.

Why damage hides at the back of base cabinets

Base cabinets sit on the subfloor with a back panel against the wall. Water from a supply line or a drain runs down that back panel and pools where you cannot see it. The sink cabinet gets checked, but the cabinet next to it can be wet for days.

Insulation sometimes sits in the wall behind the dishwasher or on an exterior wall. Once that batt is wet, it holds water against the framing and the drywall behind it.

A good inspection includes pulling the toe kick and checking the back of the neighboring cabinets with a moisture meter. A thermal imaging camera can show a cool wet patch behind a wall without cutting into it.

Kitchen water safety: the first hour

Kitchens have outlets close to sinks and several large appliances. Treat every wet surface as a possible shock risk until you know the circuit is off.

  1. Close the shut-off valve under the sink or behind the appliance. If none works, close the main water valve for the house.
  2. Mop up pooled water and lift small items off the counters and floor.
  3. Take photos of the leak source, the cabinets and the floor before moving anything.
  4. Empty the wet cabinet and set contents on a dry surface. Leave the doors open.
  5. Call the dispatch line and describe what you see.

Electrical safety around a wet kitchen

Do not stand in water and touch outlets, the dishwasher, the range or the refrigerator. If water is near the panel or the breaker box, call 911 or the electric utility before calling us.

Shut off the breaker for the wet area, and only if the panel sits on dry ground you can reach without walking through water. Unplug a refrigerator only from a dry position.

How we dry a kitchen

The crew starts with moisture meters on the cabinet floors, toe kicks and flooring, and a thermal imaging camera across the walls and floor. That map shows how far the water went and where to open up.

Standing water is removed with portable extraction units. Then the toe kick comes off, a few small holes may be drilled in cabinet backs to let air in, and air movers and a dehumidifier go to work. Wall and floor cavities get directed airflow, and readings are checked daily.

The crew keeps drying until each material's readings match its own dry standard. That standard comes from ANSI/IICRC S500, and a kitchen owner can fairly ask whether the company on site works to it. Details on the equipment side are on our structural drying page.

Contents that cannot be saved are removed and antimicrobial treatment goes on wet framing and the cabinet interior. If the water came from a dishwasher drain or a refrigerator line, the S500 category matters, and gray water changes what can stay.

Insurance and kitchen leaks

The Texas Department of Insurance lists a broken washing machine hose and a burst pipe as examples of sudden and accidental water damage that home and renters policies often cover. It also says slow leaks and seepage usually fall outside coverage, and so does any mold they cause.

That difference is why the cause of a kitchen leak matters. A line that split overnight is treated differently from a drip under the sink that went unnoticed for months. Check your policy and ask your insurer, because the wording varies.

A claim can be refused when the homeowner lets the damage grow after the leak is found. Closing the valve, drying the cabinet and keeping receipts for temporary repairs all help. The department also advises photographing damage before repairs and waiting for the insurer's inspection before permanent repairs. Our job is to document the scope, not to promise approval.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Replacing the countertop or floor before the cabinet bases are dry.
  • Running a fan across the surface and calling it done.
  • Caulking over a wet seam, which traps moisture.
  • Waiting to see whether the smell goes away.
  • Throwing out wet cabinets before the insurer or landlord has seen them.

Frequently asked questions

Can kitchen cabinets be dried in place?

Often yes, when they are plywood or solid wood and the water was clean and caught early. Particleboard that has swollen usually has to be replaced. A moisture meter reading on the cabinet floor and back panel tells the real story.

Do you have to remove the toe kick?

Usually, because water collects behind it against the subfloor. Pulling it lets air reach the cavity and lets the crew measure what is wet. It can often be reinstalled after drying if it is not swollen.

Can tile in a kitchen be saved after a leak?

Tile itself is rarely harmed, but the backer board and subfloor below can stay wet. We check the grout lines and readings. If the layers below stay wet, a section may need to be lifted.

Is a dishwasher leak covered by insurance?

A sudden failure such as a split hose is often treated as sudden and accidental, while a slow seep over months is usually excluded. Policies differ, so read yours and ask your insurer before you decide.

I rent. Who handles a kitchen leak?

Tell your landlord or property manager in writing as soon as you find it and keep a copy. The lease and your renters policy decide who pays for what, and the landlord controls repairs to the building.

Can mold grow under a kitchen sink after a leak?

Mold needs moisture, and a damp cabinet floor gives it that. Fix the leak first, then dry the area fully. If you see widespread growth, call us and we will explain the next steps.
