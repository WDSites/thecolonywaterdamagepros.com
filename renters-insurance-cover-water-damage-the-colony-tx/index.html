Renters Insurance and Water Damage in The Colony, TX

Sudden and accidental water damage to your belongings is often covered. Flood and slow seepage usually are not. Which side you land on depends on how the water got in.

Updated 2026 by The Colony Water Damage Pros

The short answer for renters in The Colony

Often yes, if the water arrived suddenly and by accident. Typical covered events, according to the Texas Department of Insurance, include a burst pipe, an overflowing toilet or a washing machine hose that breaks, under home and renters policies. A leak that has been seeping for weeks or months is usually excluded, along with the mold it produced.

Flood is a separate matter. Rising water from outside, whether from heavy rain or a lake, is not part of a standard renters policy and needs its own flood coverage. Your policy wording decides every case, so treat this page as a map for the conversation with your insurer, not as a ruling.

Your belongings are covered, the walls usually are not

A renters policy is built around personal property: furniture, clothing, electronics, a mattress, kitchenware. If a covered event soaks them, the policy may pay to repair or replace them, subject to your deductible and any limits. The drywall, flooring, cabinets and ceilings belong to the building, and the building is normally insured by the landlord or owner, not by you.

That split matters in practice. A soaked sofa goes on your claim. A swollen baseboard and a stained ceiling go to your landlord. Some renters policies also carry liability coverage, which comes into play if water from your unit damages someone else's, and that is covered separately below.

When the leak starts next door

Apartment and duplex living adds a twist: the water often comes from a unit you never enter. A cracked supply line upstairs can soak your ceiling and everything under it within hours. Your own policy may respond for your belongings, and the neighbor's liability coverage, or the building owner's, may be asked to respond too.

Sort out who reports what early. Tell your landlord in writing right away, then call your insurer. If the source was your own sink or washer, assume the liability part of your policy could be involved if a neighbor's unit suffered.

Living expenses while your unit dries

Many renters policies include an additional living expense section for when a covered event makes the home unlivable, such as lodging and extra meals. Whether it applies, how long it lasts and what it reimburses depends entirely on your policy, so ask your insurer directly before booking anything long term.

Keep receipts for every night and every meal. If the unit is only partly affected, ask whether a drying period of several days, with air movers and dehumidifiers running, counts as unlivable under your wording.

What to document before anything is moved

Texas Department of Insurance guidance is blunt about order of operations: photograph before repairs, keep damaged items for the adjuster, and do not make permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Do the following while the evidence is still wet.

  1. Shut off the main water valve or the fixture valve so the damage stops growing. Letting the damage continue after you notice it gives the insurer grounds to refuse the claim.
  2. Photograph and video each room wide, then close up: ceiling stains, wet carpet, standing water, affected furniture and electronics.
  3. List every damaged item with age, brand and where you bought it, and keep receipts or order confirmations where you have them.
  4. Keep soaked items until the adjuster has seen them, unless they are a health hazard or block safe movement.
  5. Save every message with your landlord and insurer. Note dates, names and claim numbers in one running document.
  6. Keep receipts for temporary measures such as fans, tarps or lodging.

Where mitigation fits into the claim

Drying is time sensitive, and waiting days for an adjuster can let wet materials sour. Professional water mitigation uses moisture meters, thermal imaging cameras, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers to dry the structure and contents toward the dry standard for each material. Ask your landlord, who normally controls the building, to approve and schedule that work.

Local note

Renting in The Colony: what the census says and why it matters

According to Census QuickFacts, the owner-occupied rate in The Colony is 59.8 percent, which leaves about four in ten households renting. That is a large share of the city, and it means a burst line in an apartment or duplex touches renters, landlords and neighbors at the same time.

In practice, a renter here should hold a policy of their own even if the landlord insures the building. The landlord's policy protects the structure and not your belongings, and a claim on one side does not automatically pay out on the other.

Frequently asked questions

Does renters insurance cover a burst pipe in my apartment?

Often it does for your belongings, because the Texas Department of Insurance describes a burst pipe as sudden and accidental damage. The building itself is usually the landlord's concern. Confirm the details, including your deductible, with your insurer.

Is a washing machine hose leak covered?

A broken washing machine hose is one of the examples the Texas Department of Insurance gives of covered sudden damage. If the hose was visibly worn for a long time, an insurer may look harder at whether the loss was gradual, so replace old hoses before they fail.

Does renters insurance cover flood damage?

Usually not. Flood and flood-caused mold are generally excluded and need a separate flood policy. A few policies do include some coverage, so read yours. Under a National Flood Insurance Program policy, the filing deadline is 60 days.

Will insurance pay for mold that follows water damage?

Not automatically. Mold from slow leaks is usually excluded, and most policies do not pay for mold cleanup or testing unless coverage was added. Call your insurer early and ask what your wording says about mold.

My landlord's insurance covers the building. Do I still need my own policy?

Yes, for your belongings and for liability. The owner's coverage is aimed at the structure, so your furniture, clothes and electronics fall on your side of the line.

Is there anything specific to The Colony I should check?

Yes. With about four in ten households renting here, ask your landlord which unit or building policy applies and whether the community has a standing emergency water contact. Then confirm your own flood coverage status, since this is a lakeside city where flood questions come up.
