Ceiling Water Damage Repair Cost in The Colony, TX

No two stained ceilings cost the same to fix. The price follows a short list of factors, and knowing them helps you read any scope you are handed.

Updated 2026 by The Colony Water Damage Pros

The cost of repairing a water-damaged ceiling depends on seven things. They are where the water came from, its category, how large the wet area is and what is above the drywall. The rest are the ceiling finish, whether the roof or plumbing needs its own repair and whether the board is drywall or plaster. A patch the size of a dinner plate and a collapsed living room ceiling can sit at opposite ends of the same trade.

This page gives no dollar amounts, because any figure published without seeing the ceiling would be a guess. Real numbers come from an on-site scope. What follows is how each factor changes the work, so you can compare scopes on equal terms.

The cost factors at a glance

FactorHow it changes the job
Source of the waterA one-time supply line failure is a single repair. A recurring roof or AC leak adds a second trade and sometimes a second ceiling.
Water categoryCategory 1 water may allow drying in place. Category 2 or 3 usually means removing board and insulation, then cleaning and treating framing.
Size and number of roomsMore wet area means more removal, more drying equipment and more days. Moisture often travels farther than the visible stain.
Insulation above the ceilingWet insulation holds water and is typically removed and replaced. Blown-in insulation is messier than batts.
Texture and finishPopcorn, knockdown and orange peel each have to be matched. A smooth ceiling usually means painting the whole plane.
Paint and room sizeEdge-to-edge paint avoids a visible patch line, which may extend the paint beyond the repair.
Drywall or plasterDrywall comes down in sheets. Plaster is slower to remove, harder to match and may need a specialist.

Drying equipment days, access height and whether furniture must be moved also add to labor.

Why the source matters most

A ceiling is the end of the line for water. The leak itself might be in a bathroom above, a supply line in the attic, a roof penetration or an air conditioner drain. Until it is fixed, repairing the ceiling is wasted effort, so the plumber, roofer or HVAC contractor is often the first line item.

Roof work is its own scope and its own price. If shingles, flashing or decking have failed, the restoration company dries and rebuilds the interior while a roofer handles the exterior. Keep the two estimates separate so you can see which part of the bill is which.

Category and class set the demolition

Water category follows ANSI/IICRC S500: clean, gray or black. A ceiling that took clean water from a pipe and was caught early may only need drying, a small cut-out or a skim coat. A ceiling that took sewage from a bathroom above, or water that sat in insulation for weeks, usually has to come down in the affected zone.

Class describes how much material is wet. A thermal imaging camera and moisture meter show the true wet area, which is often larger than the stain, and that size sets how many air movers and dehumidifiers run and for how many days.

Matching texture is where repairs show

A ceiling patch is easy. A patch that disappears is harder. Textured ceilings must be matched by hand or sprayed, and an older popcorn finish can be tricky to blend. When a match is poor, painters often extend the paint across the whole ceiling or room to hide the transition.

Ask whether your scope includes primer, stain blocking and the full ceiling coat. Water stains bleed through plain paint, so a sealing primer matters. Our water damage repair service covers drywall, texture and paint as one rebuild stage.

How to compare two scopes

  1. Check that both scopes assume the same water category and wet area.
  2. Confirm whether drying equipment, monitoring visits and insulation are listed.
  3. Look for texture matching and paint coverage by name.
  4. See whether roof or plumbing repair is included, excluded or left to another trade.
  5. Ask which items would change if hidden damage is found after the ceiling opens.
  6. Ask whether the company works to ANSI/IICRC S500 and records moisture readings.

Where insurance fits

The Texas Department of Insurance says home policies generally cover sudden and accidental water damage and usually exclude gradual leaks. Whether the ceiling repair is covered is a matter for your policy, and permanent repairs should wait until the insurer has inspected. Photograph the stain, keep receipts for temporary protection and call your insurer early. If an active leak needs water mitigation now, do that first.

Local note

The Colony: roof permits and ceiling repairs

Since the start of October 2025, The Colony requires a permit for roof replacement, as well as for siding replacement and foundation repair. Permits were already required for additions, alterations and repairs to existing buildings and to plumbing, electrical or mechanical systems. If your leak comes from a roof that is being replaced, that permit is part of the project, so ask the roofer who pulls it.

Because the building page never mentions water damage repair, ask the permit office at 6053 Main Street whether a ceiling rebuild in your home needs one. The city also keeps a contractor registration page worth checking before you hire for the roof or the rebuild.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the scope change after the ceiling is opened?

Water often travels along framing and pools in insulation, so the wet area behind the board can be larger than the stain. A good scope notes this and sets out what would change if more damage appears.

Is a stained ceiling that has dried safe to just paint?

Not without checking. Meter readings should show the drywall and insulation above are dry, and the leak must be fixed. Paint over wet material traps moisture and the stain tends to bleed through.

Does a roof leak repair belong to the restoration bid?

Usually not. Roof repair is a separate trade and a separate scope. The restoration company dries and rebuilds the inside, while the roofer fixes the cause.

Does replacing my roof in The Colony need a permit now?

Yes. Since October 2025 the city requires a permit for roof replacement. Ask your contractor who will pull it, and call The Colony Building Inspections to confirm requirements for any related interior repair.

What does a ceiling repair estimate depend on for a plaster ceiling?

Plaster is slower to remove and harder to match than drywall, and older plaster may need a specialist. The scope will usually need an in-person look. Ask the crew to explain how they plan to match the surface.

Can you give me a number over the phone?

Honest numbers need an on-site scope. Call the dispatch line, describe the stain, and a crew can look at the source, category and wet area. Service also covers Carrollton and Plano.
