Safety before cleanup

Power and water do not mix

Water near a panel or an outlet is an electrical emergency first. Call the electric utility or 911 before you call a restoration company.

For the appliance that failed, close its own supply valve first. Gas water heaters need extra care, so call the gas utility if you smell gas.

Move rugs, boxes and electronics off wet floors, and keep children and pets out of drain or toilet water.

HOA questions to ask early

Ask the association what it covers, what it expects you to cover, and whether contractors need to register or follow hours. If the leak came from a shared line, a roof or an exterior wall, the association may be involved in the repair.

Keep a written record of who you spoke with and when. If the loss may go to insurance, the insurer will want the cause, the timeline and the photographs.

How cleanup and drying proceed

We locate the source, classify the water under ANSI/IICRC S500, and remove standing water. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure and moisture meters verify progress, with a thermal camera helping find hidden wet spots.

Wet drywall, insulation and carpet pad are often removed. Hard flooring and framing can sometimes be dried in place. Drying ends when materials return to their dry standard, and only then does the rebuild start.

Insurance basics

According to the Texas Department of Insurance, a failed washer hose is the kind of sudden event standard policies usually answer. Slow seepage and the mold it feeds are usually excluded, and flood needs its own policy.

Report the loss soon, photograph before repairs, protect the property from more damage, and keep receipts. Ask your insurer before you approve permanent repairs.
