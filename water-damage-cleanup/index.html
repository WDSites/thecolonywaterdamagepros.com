Emergency Water Damage Cleanup in The Colony, TX

The water has stopped, but the wet material is still working against your house. We haul it out, treat what stays and document the job for your insurer, day or night.

Water is off but the floor is soaked and the house smells wrong? Our dispatch line is open 24/7, so call while you do the basics. Close the main water valve and lift valuables off the floor. Keep children and pets away from any water that came from a drain or outside, and switch off power to wet rooms only when the panel is dry and safe to reach.

Water damage cleanup in The Colony, TX is the removal of wet, contaminated or unsalvageable material after a water loss, followed by sanitizing and deodorizing what remains so the structure can dry clean.

What cleanup actually removes in the first 48 hours

Cleanup is mostly subtraction. Anything that soaked up water and cannot be dried in place quickly comes out, because wet organic material feeds odor and microbial growth within days. The first 48 hours decide how much of that subtraction you end up needing.

Typical removals on a residential job look like this:

  • Carpet pad. Foam and rebond pad hold water like a sponge and rarely dry to a clean state. Carpet itself can sometimes be lifted, cleaned and re-laid after clean water, but the pad almost always goes.
  • Wet insulation. Fiberglass batts that sag or stay damp lose their value and hold moisture against framing. Blown-in insulation in a soaked wall cavity is usually removed too.
  • Drywall cut-outs. Crews cut above the visible wet line so the wall cavity can be opened and dried. Wicking can carry moisture above what you can see.
  • Baseboards and trim. Pulled to release trapped water and to give air movers access behind the wall.
  • Debris and ruined contents. Swollen particleboard furniture, soaked cardboard and wet upholstery that cannot be saved.

Water categories decide how aggressive cleanup gets

The industry standard ANSI/IICRC S500 sorts water into three categories, and the category sets the rules for what can be kept. A clean supply line break and a sewage backup are not the same job, even if both leave you with a wet floor.

Water also changes category with time. Clean water that sits for days in a warm house can degrade, which is one more reason cleanup should not wait for the weekend.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat it means for cleanup
Category 1 (clean)Broken supply line, tub overflow, rainwater from a clean sourcePorous items often dry in place. Pad and wet insulation still come out if they cannot dry fast.
Category 2 (gray)Washing machine discharge, dishwasher overflow, toilet overflow with urine onlyAffected pad and carpet are usually discarded. Hard surfaces get cleaned and treated with antimicrobial.
Category 3 (black)Sewage backup, river or lake flooding, long-standing waterPorous materials are removed and discarded. Crews wear protective gear and the area is cleaned, treated and dried.

Your restorer assigns the category on site after looking at the source and how long materials stayed wet. Ask to be told which category your job is.

Sanitizing, antimicrobial treatment and odor

Once the wet material is gone, the remaining surfaces get cleaned. Crews scrub framing, subfloor and concrete, then apply an antimicrobial treatment suited to the category. This step is about the surface you will be living behind, not a cosmetic wipe.

Odor control follows the cleanup, not the other way around. Spraying fragrance over wet carpet only hides the problem for a day or two. Real deodorizing means the source material is out and the area is dry, then HEPA air scrubbers and antimicrobial treatment can address what lingers in the air and on the framing.

A smell that returns after the room felt dry usually means moisture is still sitting somewhere. That is a drying question, and our water mitigation work uses moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to find it.

Contents, pack-out and what to keep or discard

Pack-out simply means moving your belongings out of the affected rooms, sometimes to storage, so crews can work and so items are not re-wetted. Furniture legs on wet carpet leave stains and swelling within hours, so lifting pieces onto foil, plastic or blocks is a good first move if you can do it safely.

Keep or discard decisions follow the water category and the material. Use this as a starting point, then let the crew confirm on site.

Salvage is never promised before the item is inspected. Keep damaged items for your adjuster until they say otherwise.

Documenting the loss for your insurer

Cleanup removes the evidence, so photograph first. The Texas Department of Insurance advises taking photos before repairs start, keeping damaged items for the adjuster to see and keeping receipts for temporary repairs. Do not make permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected.

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, a toilet overflow or a failed washing machine hose, is often covered under home and renters policies. Gradual leaks usually are not. Flood is typically excluded and needs a separate flood policy, so check your policy before you assume.

Report the loss soon after you find it, because some policies set short deadlines. A claim can also be refused when the owner let the loss keep growing, which is why stopping the water and starting cleanup matter on paper as well as in the house.

Two ways to handle cleanup, with honest trade-offs

A small Category 1 spill, a few square feet of wet carpet, can be handled by a homeowner with a wet vac, fans and a quick call to a restorer for moisture readings. That is a reasonable first response.

Anything larger, anything that touched a wall cavity, or anything Category 2 or 3 is a different job. Hiring a professional crew costs more up front but it brings extraction units, air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and meters, and it leaves a written record. Cutting corners here often means paying twice when hidden moisture turns into a rebuild. When cleanup ends and repair begins

Cleanup leaves you with a clean, dry, open structure. It does not leave you with finished walls. Once drying is complete, rebuilding the drywall, flooring and paint falls under water damage repair, and keeping the two stages with one crew or one plan avoids gaps in the paper trail.

Call the dispatch line at night or on a weekend the same way you would at noon. Same-day response is offered when available, and a crew can start the wet-material removal that limits how far the damage spreads.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle water damage cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends?

Yes. Calls are taken around the clock, holidays included, and crews offer same-day response when available. If the water is still running, shut the main valve first, then call so crews can plan what to bring.

What is the difference between water damage cleanup and water mitigation?

Cleanup removes wet debris and contaminated material and sanitizes what remains. Mitigation is the wider job of stopping the spread, which includes drying with air movers and dehumidifiers. On most losses the two overlap, and one crew often does both.

Can I keep my carpet after water damage?

Sometimes, if the water was clean and the carpet is lifted and dried fast. The pad underneath is usually replaced regardless. After Category 2 or 3 water, carpet is normally discarded.

Will my insurance pay for cleanup?

That comes down to your policy and what caused the loss. A pipe that bursts suddenly is the easier case, a slow leak is harder, and flood sits under its own policy. Take photos first, set aside ruined items for the adjuster and get your insurer's view before permanent repairs.

Do you remove sewage and Category 3 water?

Yes, with protective gear and containment where needed. Keep everyone out of the area, do not run fans that spread contamination and call the restoration line. Porous materials that touched the water are removed, and the remaining surfaces are cleaned and treated.

How long until the house smells normal again?

It depends on how fast wet material came out and how long drying takes. Odor fades once the source is removed and moisture readings return to the dry standard for each material. A lingering smell after cleanup usually points to hidden moisture, which meters and thermal cameras can locate.
