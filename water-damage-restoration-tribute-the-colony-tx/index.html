Water Damage Restoration in The Tribute, The Colony, TX

The Tribute sits on a peninsula in Lewisville Lake, which changes how storms, gates and insurance questions play out when water gets inside.

A peninsula changes the weather story

Lewisville Lake began collecting water in 1954 after the Corps of Engineers finished the dam, according to the city's own history page. The Tribute sits on a peninsula reaching into that lake, so lake level is part of the local picture alongside storms and plumbing.

In late May 2015 the lake rose to a record 537.02 feet, reported by The Dallas Morning News. CBS News reported that the Corps closed the lake to boaters because large wakes could flood nearby homes. Those reports were about lakeside homes in other cities, and we found no report that Tribute homes took water. The lesson is practical: lake level is a real variable, and it is not the same thing as a plumbing leak.

Most water damage calls anywhere in The Colony still start inside the house, from a supply line, a water heater, a roof opening or an air handler. A lake address does not change that, but it does change which insurance policy answers.

Flood or plumbing, because the policy decides

The Texas Department of Insurance explains that standard home policies cover sudden and accidental water damage from things like a burst pipe or a broken washer hose. They do not cover flood, and flood generally needs a separate policy, with claims on federal flood policies filed within 60 days.

That split matters on a lake. If surface water comes in from outside, you are in flood territory. If a pipe fails in the wall, you are in plumbing territory. Tell the insurer plainly which one happened, photograph everything before repairs, and keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them.

Gradual seepage and the mold that follows are usually excluded, so a slow leak you ignored is a harder claim than a pipe that split overnight. Read your own policy and ask your insurer about both.

Safety steps before anyone enters

Electrical first, then water

Keep out of any room where water has reached a panel, an outlet or an appliance. The electric utility or 911 comes first, before a restoration call.

Close the main water valve if reaching it is safe. Cut power to wet rooms only from a dry spot, never while standing in water.

Water from a toilet, a drain or the outdoors can carry contamination. Keep children and pets away and wear boots and gloves if you must cross it.

Gates, schedules and getting crews in

Gated enclaves add a practical step that people forget in the stress of a leak. Contact your association or gate attendant so a restoration crew can get in, and ask whether there are rules about trucks, equipment and hours.

Dry-out equipment runs for days, not minutes. Air movers and dehumidifiers need a power source, a clear path and a place to stage, so tell us about stairs, garages and anything that limits access.

How we approach the drying

A job begins with a category and a moisture map. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference for the three water categories and four classes, and it is the standard to ask any contractor about.

Standing water comes out first, using pumps or truck-mounted and portable extraction units. Then air movers and dehumidifiers run while moisture meters and a thermal camera track what is still wet, and drying continues until each material reaches its dry standard.

Hardwood, wet drywall and insulation get separate decisions. Some are dried in place, some are removed, and the readings decide which.

What not to do

  • Do not assume a lake address means the policy covers flood. Check it.
  • Do not run electrical equipment in standing water.
  • Do not wait for a gate list or a schedule before you photograph damage.

Did Tribute homes flood in 2015?

We found no report that they did. The reports we found describe record lake levels and boating closures because of wake concerns in other lakeside areas.

What if the water came from the lake and not a pipe?

That is a flood loss, and standard home policies generally exclude it. Ask your insurer about any separate flood policy, and note that federal flood claims have a 60 day filing window.

Can you work around the golf course and HOA rules?

We work with the access rules your association sets. Tell the association what you are doing and when, so equipment and crews are not turned away at the gate.
